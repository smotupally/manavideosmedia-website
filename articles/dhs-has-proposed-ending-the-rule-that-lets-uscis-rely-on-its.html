DHS has proposed ending the rule that lets USCIS rely on its earlier approval when the same employer extends the same worker in the same job.

Under the proposal, titled "Removing Deference to Prior USCIS Determinations of Eligibility," an extension would be reviewed as if USCIS had never approved it before. The rule covers Form I-129 petitions — H-1B, L-1, O-1, TN and others. DHS estimates about 208,000 petitions a year could be affected.

DHS says the change would put the burden of proof back on employers, encourage officers to ask for more evidence, and stop errors in an old approval from carrying forward. Its own data show denial rates did not change when deference was removed before — the measurable effect was more Requests for Evidence: about 6,044 extra a year, each adding roughly 64 days.

The public comment period closed on November 13, with 1,780 submissions received. The current rule is still in force — nothing changes until DHS publishes a final rule.

For now, workers on extensions can generally keep working for up to 240 days after their stay expires while a timely-filed extension is pending — but slower processing could push more people closer to that limit.
