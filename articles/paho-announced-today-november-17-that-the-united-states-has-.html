PAHO announced today, November 17, that the United States has officially lost its measles elimination status — a designa

2026-11-17•2 min read•By Mana Videos Media
Share this story: WhatsApp X Facebook

PAHO announced today, November 17, that the United States has officially lost its measles elimination status — a designation it held since 2000. The virus has now spread without a break for more than a year, which means measles is considered endemic in the US again.

The CDC has confirmed 5,412 measles cases in 2026 across 45 states, with 10 deaths reported by states this year. More than 90% of cases have been in people who were unvaccinated or whose vaccination status is unknown. Mexico and Bolivia also lost their elimination status today; Canada lost its status in 2025.

To regain the status, the US must submit a corrective action plan to PAHO and show a full year with no endemic spread. Two doses of the MMR vaccine are about 97% effective, and health officials say about 95% of a community needs to be vaccinated to stop outbreaks — kindergarten coverage in the US is now about 92%.

If you have young children, check their MMR vaccination records with your pediatrician — babies can get an early dose at 6 months if there is an outbreak in your area.
