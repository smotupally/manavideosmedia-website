A federal judge in Boston on Tuesday, November 17, blocked the Department of Homeland Security from enforcing its August limits on Curricular Practical Training (CPT) — the program that lets F-1 students take internships and practical training for academic credit.

The two directives, issued by the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP) on August 12 and August 24, told universities that internships qualify for CPT only if they are required for every student in a degree program. That cut out elective, credit-bearing internships — the kind most students use. U.S. District Judge Julia E. Kobick found DHS likely acted unlawfully by reversing a reading in place for more than 35 years without acknowledging or explaining the change.

The case, AAU v. DHS, was filed on October 5 by four higher-education groups — the Association of American Universities, NAFSA, the Presidents' Alliance and the Association of Independent Colleges and Universities in Massachusetts. In 2024, 140,775 student visa holders were authorized for CPT, according to ICE data; Amazon and Tesla were the top employers. Columbia University said it had denied CPT to more than 120 students since August, and approvals at the University of Wisconsin fell 60 percent.

The order is preliminary, and the government is expected to appeal. It does not force any university to reopen elective CPT — each school's designated school official (DSO) decides — so students should check with their DSO before making internship plans. The ruling came one day after a different Boston judge struck down the DHS rule ending duration of status for F-1 and J-1 students.
