India Questions US Panel Over Silence on Attacks Against Indians in America

India's Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) on Tuesday, November 10, said the United States Commission on International Religious Freedom (USCIRF) has stayed silent on attacks on places of worship and hate crimes targeting the Indian community in the United States — even as the panel comments on religious freedom in India.

MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal called the USCIRF a "biased and ill-informed organisation" with a "blatantly political agenda" at a media briefing in New Delhi, and said "prejudiced entities like the USCIRF can hardly be taken seriously."

The remarks came after two USCIRF commissioners raised concerns last week about alleged attacks on gurdwaras, churches and mosques in India. Jaiswal pointed to the alleged plot to attack the Sri Venkateswara Swami (Balaji) Temple in Aurora, Illinois, on October 11 — when police say Alexandros Paporakis, 19, tried to open fire before his rifle jammed. 105 US lawmakers have urged the FBI to investigate it as a possible federal hate crime.

A September 2026 study cited by the lawmakers found more than 542,000 anti-South Asian hateful posts on X between January 2025 and June 2026.
