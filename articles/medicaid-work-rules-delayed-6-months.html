Medicaid Work Rules Delayed 6 Months

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) announced on Friday, November 13, that states will not deny or drop anyone from Medicaid over the new work requirements until July 1, 2027.

CMS created an optional six-month 'hold harmless' period running through June 30, 2027. States that use it will still check whether people meet the requirement or qualify for an exclusion — but no one who is otherwise eligible can lose coverage, or be denied coverage, solely for failing to meet it.

The requirements still take effect on January 1, 2027, for many Medicaid applicants and beneficiaries ages 19 to 64. People can qualify through 80 hours a month of work, community service or a work program; enrollment in an educational program at least half-time; a combination totaling at least 80 hours a month; or meeting a monthly income threshold. States must ask for the flexibility in their mitigation plans by December 11.

About 19 million people — able-bodied adults without young children covered through Medicaid expansion — fall under the requirements, and the Congressional Budget Office has estimated about 5 million fewer people will be on Medicaid a decade from now because of them.
