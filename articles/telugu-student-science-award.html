Telugu Student's Innovation Wins National Science Award

జాతీయ సైన్స్ అవార్డు గెలుచుకున్న తెలుగు విద్యార్థి
Oct 13, 20262 min readBy Mana Videos Media
Community News

HOUSTON, TX — A 16-year-old Houston high school student has won a national science award for a low-cost water purification device designed for rural schools — a project inspired by his family's roots in Telangana.

The student's prototype uses inexpensive, locally available materials to filter contaminants from drinking water, bringing the per-unit cost far below commercial alternatives. Judges praised both the engineering and the project's clear real-world purpose: safe drinking water for schools that cannot afford conventional purification systems.

The idea took shape during a family trip to Telangana, where the student visited a village school and saw students drinking from an unreliable water source. "I kept thinking about it after we came home," he said. Back in Houston, he spent months testing designs in his school's lab with guidance from his science teacher.

The award includes scholarship support and an invitation to present the project at a national young-innovators showcase next spring. The student says his next goal is to pilot the device in a handful of Telangana schools in partnership with a nonprofit working in the region.

Community members in Houston celebrated the win as another example of Telugu-American students making their mark in STEM. "Our kids are solving real problems," said one family friend. "That is something to be proud of."
