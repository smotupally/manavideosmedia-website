India Expects 'Best Deal' in US Trade Pact, Says Goyal

India Expects 'Best Deal' in US Trade Pact, Says Goyal

Union Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal said India has negotiated a 'very good' set of outcomes in the proposed trade pact with the United States — and that India will have the best deal, with preferential access over its competitors. He was speaking at the 31st CII Partnership Summit in Visakhapatnam on Thursday, November 12.

Goyal said the nine free-trade agreements India has signed in the last five years give Indian businesses open access to 38 countries — a global economy worth $85 trillion, about two-thirds of the world economy — and that talks in the pipeline could add another $15 trillion in market access. He also said a bilateral trade pact with the European Union could be signed by the end of the year, and that India is looking at an equitable deal with Canada, with no timeframe given.

Calling India "the safest destination for global investments in a turbulent world," Goyal invited global companies to invest in India and Andhra Pradesh. The two-day summit in Visakhapatnam has drawn policymakers and business leaders from nearly 60 countries.

Talks with the US continue — the two sides announced a framework for an interim agreement in February 2026, and a US team was in New Delhi on November 2-3 for technical discussions. No signing date has been announced, and nothing changes for exporters until a deal is signed.
