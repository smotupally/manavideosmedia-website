31,383 Comments Flood In Against Ending H-1B Grace Period

31,383 Comments Flood In Against Ending H-1B Grace Period

The Trump administration's proposal to scrap the 60-day grace period for laid-off H-1B workers drew 31,383 public comments by Tuesday night, November 10, when the comment window closed. The count had risen from about 27,000 on November 2 to 30,382 early Tuesday.

The Department of Homeland Security published the proposal on September 11, 2026. It would remove the rule that lets workers in H-1B, L-1, O-1 and TN status — and their dependents — stay up to 60 days after a job ends to find new work, change status, or prepare to leave. About 70% of approved H-1B petitions are for beneficiaries born in India, according to USCIS figures cited in its fiscal 2025 report to Congress.

In a separate development, a coalition of 24 state attorneys general led by California Attorney General Rob Bonta filed a formal letter with DHS on Monday, November 9, urging the department to withdraw its proposal to charge universities up to $100,000 per international student for Optional Practical Training (OPT) — $70,000 for an initial recommendation and $30,000 for a subsequent one. That proposal drew 8,947 public comments before its window closed on November 9. The coalition argues the charge exceeds DHS's legal authority and calls it an "unlawful tax" — its words, not ours. It also says the rule could cost institutions $12.9 billion a year if participation held steady.

Nothing changes yet: DHS must review the comments before deciding whether to issue a final rule, and the 60-day grace period stays in effect until any final rule takes effect. A third proposal — to stop generally deferring to prior USCIS approvals on extensions — drew 686 comments, with its deadline on November 13.
